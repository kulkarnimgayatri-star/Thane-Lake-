This journey began as a 42-week exploration, documenting the incredible lakes of Thane every Sunday. From Masunda Lake to Kamal Lake, we studied and shared the forgotten stories of these water bodies.

Thane has long been known as the “City of Lakes”, with a history spanning nearly 10–12 centuries. During the Shilahara period, King Bimba Raja chose Thane as the capital of Sashti for its natural richness, digging lakes in every locality for water supply. Temples of village deities were often built nearby, a tradition continued during the Yadav period.

In modern times, clean-up efforts have unearthed ancient idols, sculptures, and inscriptions—such as the beautiful Brahma idol at Siddheshwar Lake and the Sheshashayi Vishnu idol and inscription at Kasarvadavali. These discoveries, now studied by the Archaeology Department, reveal profound historical details. While lakes like Masunda, Kacharli, Siddheshwar, Upavan, and Khidkali remain culturally significant, many others are disappearing due to urbanization, construction, and encroachments.

Our goal is simple: to spread awareness about their heritage.
